LoRA and its variants improve fine-tuning efficiency by constraining trainable updates to a low-rank form \cite{hu2022lora}. Subsequent methods adapt the rank budget, initialization, or learning-rate scaling, including AdaLoRA, DoRA, LoRA+, GaLore, PiSSA, and LoRA-GA \cite{zhang2023adalora,liu2024dora,hayou2024loraplus,zhao2024galore,meng2024pissa,wang2024loraga}. These methods address capacity allocation, optimization scaling, or memory, but they generally optimize a particular factor representative. Our focus is orthogonal: we study the exact $\mathrm{GL}(r)$ symmetry of the factorization and ask which optimizer components are invariant to this symmetry.

Riemannian optimization on fixed-rank matrices provides the mathematical background for quotient geometries, horizontal spaces, and retractions \cite{absil2008optimization,mishra2014fixedrank,boumal2023introduction,vandereycken2013completion,bonnabel2013sgd}. Riemannion move the trainable object toward the product-space fixed-rank manifold, where parametrization ambiguity is removed by construction \cite{bogachev2025riemannlora}. Intrinsic Muon is closer to a norm-ball or product-space optimization view, emphasizing matrix-norm geometry and a linear-minimization-oracle style direction \cite{li2026intrinsicmuon}. Our construction also belongs to fixed-rank Riemannian optimization. We specify the metric induced by the balanced factor manifold, retain the factor interface, and analyze the connection together with persistent first-moment state. Table~\ref{tab:related-work} compares these mechanisms under explicit feature definitions.

This distinction matters for both method and interpretation. Embedded product formulations remove factor ambiguity at the variable level; quotient formulations instead retain a choice of representative and a horizontal lift. Neither mechanism by itself establishes that removing the vertical component of an adaptive factor update improves a task metric. Our connection-based view retains the factors, decomposes optimizer updates into horizontal and vertical components via a Sylvester equation, and then tests balance retraction, horizontal projection, and horizontal momentum separately. The resulting message is more diagnostic than a generic Riemannian-optimizer comparison: balance is theorem-enforced to fix the representative conditioning, while the empirical value of horizontal projection depends on the regime.

There is also a broad literature on symmetries, implicit bias, and deep linear dynamics \cite{badrinarayanan2015symmetries,saxe2014exact,arora2020deep,arora2020greedy,soltanolkotabi2025implicit,hardt2016train}. These works show that parameterization can shape optimization even when the represented function class is unchanged. We add a concrete diagnostic for LoRA: the raw gradient is horizontal, but adaptive optimizer transformations can introduce vertical update energy. The vertical component is invisible to the product differential but contributes mixed terms at finite step size. Its trajectory effect depends on the actual state dynamics.
