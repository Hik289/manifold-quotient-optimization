\begin{abstract}
Low-rank factors carry a general-linear symmetry that makes optimizer state depend on the chosen representative. We construct the balanced quotient geometry, derive its product-space metric and gradient, and combine coordinate transport with a Sylvester projection of persistent first-moment state. A second-order pullback identity yields the sharp Hessian bound $2bL_f+\|\nabla f\|_2$; exact residual-gap and deep-chain spectra lead to Schur momentum rates and a Lyapunov-certified local basin. Hessian whitening gives finite-sample risk bounds with quadratic-model coefficient $1/2$, and an identifiable homoscedastic sensing model has leading risk $\sigma_\varepsilon^2r(m+d-r)/(2n)$, matching its Fisher information. Experiments include 208 matched-budget task-search attempts and 36 frozen-configuration initialization checks: across three model initializations, mean validation-selected test loss decreases by $22.6\%$ on Cora and $82.2\%$ on synthetic SDF fitting relative to AdamW. Twelve-gauge sensing tests attain product-trajectory disagreement at most $5.2\times10^{-5}$, and complete loss trajectories connect the geometric analysis with measured optimization behavior.
\end{abstract}
